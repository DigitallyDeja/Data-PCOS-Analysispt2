# Data-PCOS-Analysispt2
This is a follow up to the <a href="https://github.com/DigitallyDeja/Data-PCOS-Analysis">Data-PCOS-Analysis part one project</a>. This project is utilizing the previous Excel data and it is extracted through Tableau. The KPIs highlights a different angle in business questions, and as a result this alters analysis priorities.

## Questions KPIs
### Summary KPIs
- What is the total of participants?
    
### Core KPIs
- What is the PCOS Status among participants (Yes, No, Not diagnosed)?
- Does exercising (Exerciser, Non-exerciser) have a contribution to heart disease for PCOS participants?
- Is there a correlation with the participants' mental health and hormonal imbalances?
- How does taking PCOS medication affect conception amongst different age groups?

## Project Insights 
- There are **173** participants in total across all age groups.
- **21.97%** of participants have PCOS in the dataset, while **77.46%** do not.
- According to the data **~21%** of PCOS participants that do not exercise are diagnosed with heart disease, and less than **3%** who exercise is diagnosed. Whereas **~95%** of PCOS participants exercise, the data shows that exercise drastically decreases the likelihood of heart disease.
- **92%** of PCOS particpants that have hormonal imbalances struggle with mental health, this is a strong indicator that PCOS affects mental health
- Medication use is highest at **56.76%** among **20-25** year olds, with most users not having diagnosed conception difficulty. This suggests PCOS medication is primarily used for hormonal regulation rather than fertility treatment. Medication usage actually decreases in older age groups **(25-30: 8.11%; 30-35: 2.70%)** where fertility concerns typically peak. This reveals no strong correlation between medication use and conception difficulty across age groups, indicating that further analysis is needed to distinguish between medication types and understand treatment patterns among those actively experiencing conception complications.

### Conclusion
   This analysis of **173** participants reveals significant lifestyle and health impacts associated with PCOS, with nearly **22%** having a confirmed diagnosis. Exercise shows as a critical factor, as non-exercisers show dramatically higher cardiovascular disease rates **(95.83%)** compared to exercisers **(21.43%)**. Mental health is substantially impacted among those with hormonal imbalance, emphasizing the interconnected nature of PCOS symptoms. Additionally, medication use peaks at **56.76%** among **20-25** year olds, with most users not having diagnosed conception difficulty. This suggests medications primarily address hormonal regulation rather than fertility in this dataset. Usage decreases in older age groups **(25-30: 8.11%; 30-35: 2.70%)**, revealing no strong correlation between medication and conception difficulty. These findings underscore that PCOS management requires a comprehensive approach integrating exercise intervention, cardiovascular monitoring, mental health support, and age-appropriate medication strategies, with further research needed to understand fertility-specific treatment patterns across different life stages.
